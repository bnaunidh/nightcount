# SECONDARY PROMPT — DON'T MAKE EVERYTHING A CUTSCENE

Read alongside the master prompt and the addicting prompt. Supplied by the
author; kept here verbatim in substance because it decides how every scripted
moment in this game gets built.

## 1. The rule

Most of the dialogue and most of the scripted moments should play out in-engine,
in first person, with the player still holding the controls. The night scripts
are written cinematically because that is how you write a script. **That does
not mean they get shot as cinematics.** A line of dialogue in a design document
is not an instruction to take the camera away.

Every time you take control off the player you are telling them "this part isn't
yours." In a first-person horror game that is fatal, because the fear lives
entirely in being the one standing there.

## 2. Default: the player keeps control

- the mouse and the movement keys stay theirs
- dialogue is positional audio with subtitles, from someone actually in the room
- the player's own lines never cut away from their own eyes
- they can look away, walk off, or ignore it, and the game keeps going
- beats are staged with light, sound, movement and blocking — not by forcing the
  camera

*A moment the player can look away from and chooses not to is worth ten they
were held in place for.*

## 3. Not cutscenes

Customer conversations and every three-option dialogue. The player's own
muttering. Finding things — the tally, the folders, the fourth aisle, a receipt.
Most scares. Anything the player has already understood. Transitions between
tasks.

## 4. Worth a cutscene

The arrival at the top of a night. A moment where **losing control is the
content**. A jump in time or place the player cannot walk. The ending, once.
That is roughly the whole budget.

## 5. If you must cut away

Seconds, not minutes. First person where possible. Never more than about fifteen
seconds without giving something back. Skippable on a repeat, and skipping must
never break a script, a flag or progression. Return control cleanly.

## 6. The test

> "Would this be scarier, sadder, or more interesting if I were the one standing
> there with the mouse in my hand?"

If yes — and it usually is — it isn't a cutscene.
