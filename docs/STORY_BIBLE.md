# THE NIGHT COUNT — Story Bible

**Canonical.** This document describes the game as it now exists, written to
the storyline the author supplied across six nights. It replaces every earlier
version, including the Wren Alcott fiction and the Jacob-only rewrite that
followed it. Where anything else in `docs/` disagrees with this, this wins.

Everything the player sees must be consistent with what is written here.

---

## 1. The one-line version

A man takes a night job at a filling station that is closing in a week, because
his friend went missing out here and he has never stopped looking. Over six
nights the station teaches him that he is not looking from the outside.

## 2. The player character

**Danny.** Twenties, broke, not an investigator and not brave. He took the job
for the money and because of **Jacob**.

The game does not name him for five nights. He is never labelled in dialogue —
his own lines are unlabelled captions, the way you do not hear your own name in
your head. **The first time the game says it is on a note from his boss on
Night 6** — *"Danny —"* — and the second time is a doctor saying it.

He talks to himself, out loud, the way people do on a long shift alone. Short
lines. Dry when he is coping. Flat when he is not. He carries a photograph of
himself and Jacob squinting into the sun; it goes in his pocket before the
first shift and stays there.

## 3. What is objectively true

- The station is closing, and the closure is being rushed.
- People have gone missing on this stretch of road, repeatedly, unreported.
- Jacob is one of them, and Jacob is present in the building. He is not a
  hallucination, a dream, or grief — the game never suggests otherwise while
  it is happening, and never walks it back.
- **Salt works.** A line holds. What it stops does not leave; it goes around.
- The blood in the restroom is real and, on Night 2, still warm.
- **Danny has been at this station far longer than six nights.** His car is
  under a cover in the back lot. His employee file is in the drawer with a hire
  date years before the week he thinks he has worked.
- **He is in a coma, and has been for ten years.** The crash on the highway
  outside the station is the crash he investigates on Night 3, hears on Night 1,
  and dies into on Night 6. Jacob was in the car with him and did not survive.

## 4. What is deliberately ambiguous

- Whether Jacob is warning him or calling him in.
- Whether the six nights happened, in any sense, or are ten years of a sleeping
  man's night shift.
- **The boy fixes the ending against the cheapest reading of it.** A coma
  ending risks "none of it counted". But a seventeen-year-old has been visiting
  every Tuesday since he was small, and says Danny got him out of somewhere
  once, and could never explain it in a way anyone understood. Danny cannot
  have invented him. So either something real happened out there, or the
  kindest thing he did in ten years of sleeping reached the outside anyway.
  **The game never says which, and does not have to.**

## 5. The shape of the week

| | Night | The job | What it costs him |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | the job | clean, stock, open | 02:36 — a screech and two bangs he doesn't go out for |
| 2 | Jacob | the rain, the blood trail | he goes down on the tile; *"…Then why is it still warm."* |
| 3 | the salt | six pours, nine doors | 01:58 — go out or stay in; both cost him |
| 4 | the sleep | ten minutes, he says | two hours in the dark, and a woman is gone |
| 5 | the storm | one pour, nine doors | a boy in the aisle who is forty miles away |
| 6 | Sunday | six pours, six doors | everything |

### The clock

| Time | Phase |
|---|---|
| ~17:50 | Arrival. He parks. The radio cuts. He talks to himself in the car. |
| 18:00–21:00 | **PREP.** Clean it, stock it, open it. |
| **21:00** | **Open the station.** |
| 21:00–06:00 | **SHIFT.** Serve whoever comes in. |
| **06:00** | **Close up.** |

Nights 4 and 6 do not reach 06:00. Night 4 ends at **02:24**, three and a half
hours early, with the shift abandoned and the watch buzzing to say so. Night 6
ends at **01:41**, on the road.

## 6. The six nights

### Night 1 — the job, and one thing that is not the job

Cold open in the car. The radio cuts mid-song — **that song matters, and
nothing is made of it here**. The closure, the disappearances, the photograph.
*"…One of them was my friend."*

Prep, then the shift. Two things do not belong: **the blood under the mess on
the restroom floor**, which nobody comments on, and **02:36**, when a screech
and a bang happen outside and the task list does not tell him to look.

### Night 2 — Jacob

Rain. In a lightning flash: **Jacob, in the rain, covered in blood.** One
frame. Blood on the forecourt leads inside, down the hall, into the restroom,
where Jacob is facing the wall. A second flash and he is gone and Danny is on
the tile.

> *"…ow."* … *"There's nothing there. There's nothing there."*
> *"…Then why is it still warm."*

At **03:14**, from inside the building, in Jacob's voice: *"come here."*

### Night 3 — the salt

He brings a bag. **Six pours, nine thresholds** — and the shortfall is never
explained. At 21:40 there is a tally scratched in the grout at chest height:
four marks and a fifth struck across them. He does not clean it off.

At 23:12 a man with no car pays five dollars for nothing and says **"Don't do
five."** At **01:58**, a scream and two shots, and no task appears:

- **He goes out** — a car nose-down in the ditch, headlights still on, two
  casings, and a bag of shopping with a receipt from this station **timestamped
  tomorrow**. And because he was out there, the front door is the one he did
  not salt, and something is standing in an aisle when he gets back.
- **He stays in** — at 02:06 all four pumps run themselves with the nozzles in
  the cradles and stop at exactly **$19.72**.

At the door, before he locks up: *"Maybe some salt will help next time."*

At 06:00: the line undisturbed, **and footprints on both sides of it.**

### Night 4 — the sleep

No rain. The lot is quiet in a way the last three weren't. The bag is lighter
than it was — **whatever he did not pour on Night 3 is what he has tonight**.

At 00:19 he puts his head down. *"Ten minutes. Just ten."*

**The screen goes black and the game does not skip.** The chime. Two sets of
footsteps, one adult and one small and quick. A chip bag. Notes on the
laminate. *"I'm going to the bathroom. You better not steal my cash like the
last dude did."* A door closing at 00:21. And a boy humming, somewhere, in
the dark — **the song off the radio on Night 1**.

At **02:16** a scream. The money is on the counter. Small fists on the
restroom door. *"She's been in there ages —"* — *"…Ages?"* — *"Since when you
were sleeping!"* She went in at 00:21.

Three wrong keys, then the fourth. It is standing over her, and this time it is
looking at him. **He throws the salt himself.** It screams, the light blows
out, and there is a coat he recognises and a shoe and very little else.

He puts the boy in the car with the heater on, goes back in for eleven seconds,
does not touch the money, clocks out at 02:24, and lets the watch buzz. On the
way out the restroom door is open, the light is on, and the floor is clean.

Beside him in the car, the kid is humming. Same four notes.

### Night 5 — the storm

The passenger seat is empty; the boy is at his aunt's, forty miles away, and
fine. **He cannot save anyone tonight, and that is the point.** He turns the
bag upside down: **enough for one.** *"One door."*

The storm closes in by the hour — eleven seconds between flash and bang at
nine, four at midnight, and by three they arrive together.

- **22:30** — there are four aisles. There have always been three. The fire
  plan on the office wall, older than he is, shows four. Every brand is one
  nobody makes; every expiry is a year that hasn't happened.
- **23:47** — the man with the five, and it is the *same five*, still on the
  counter from Tuesday. *"You did five."* … *"You're not the first one to be
  kind, son. You're the sixth."* No car. No footprints either way.
- **01:31** — six marks in the grout now, fresh, and the wall is **warm**.
- **02:16** — nothing happens. For four minutes. Then the drawer opens on its
  own and closes again, and that is all, and he laughs once and it doesn't
  sound like him.
- **03:14** — every light dies, including the fridges, which have never
  stopped. **The flashlight is not where he left it.** In three flashes: the
  boy at the end of the aisle, soaked in blood, one arm gone, staring; closer;
  running, flat out, silent. Then every light in the building comes on, the
  aisle is empty, and there is a patch of blood the size of a seven-year-old
  spreading from nothing.
- **The call** — the aunt's number rings and picks up. Breathing, and four
  notes hummed. Then: number not recognised. Twice. In the office he finds
  Jacob's employee file — night attendant, four years ago, no termination date
  — and five folders with no name on the tab. **And his. Six.**
- The phone on the desk rings. **It has no cord.**

At 06:00 the storm has stopped. On the passenger seat there is a bag of salt,
full, unopened, that he did not put there, and under it a receipt timestamped
tomorrow. He drives to the aunt's and sits on the floor next to a sleeping
child with both arms and puts his face in his hands.

### Night 6 — Sunday

Dead still. The radio is already off. **Six pours, and for the first time,
exactly six doors.** The player finally has enough, and that should feel wrong.

- **21:04** — the note. *"Danny —"* … *"You **were** a good one."*
- **23:15** — the cover is off the car in the back lot. It is a Nova. It is
  his: his registration, the dent he put in it at nineteen, the air freshener
  from another state. On the passenger seat, the photograph that is in his
  pocket right now. He checks. It is. *"…How long have I been here."*
- **01:30** — every light out. Thunder, with no storm. The beam finds Jacob,
  covered in dry blood, face doing nothing.

  > *"Da— Danny…"* … *"I waited."* … *"You said you'd come get me after your
  > shift."* … *"You said ten minutes."* … **"Danny, why didn't you wake up."**

- **01:31** — ⚠ GET OUT. The salt does nothing; it walks over every line laid
  all week. The fourth aisle is full of people facing the shelves. The door
  chime goes off cheerfully, which is the worst sound in the game.
- **01:41** — ten minutes of road. He checks the mirror twice. *"…I think I
  lost him."* The radio comes on by itself, mid-song. **Four notes.** Headlights.

Rain on metal. Wipers going. A door hanging open. A bag of shopping with a
receipt in it. **And somewhere behind, at the station, a man on his shift hears
a scream and two bangs and stands very still and doesn't come out.**

## 7. The ending

Credits roll in silence — no music for ninety seconds, only a heart monitor
mixed so low most players will not notice when it starts. Nothing hints that
anything follows.

Then white, resolving slowly, the way waking up actually does. **Noah**, a
doctor in his fifties, sitting down on the edge of the bed like his legs have
gone. *"Danny, it's been ten years."*

The first input since the crash moves Danny's hand and **is mapped to nothing**.

Jacob was in the car. There is a young man who has come every Tuesday since he
was small, who says Danny got him out of somewhere once, who is seventeen now,
and who is in the corridor.

*"Welcome back, Danny."* The door opens. Someone is humming. Four notes.

## 8. Rules for writing in this world

- **He talks to himself, and only to himself.** No narrator.
- **Nobody explains anything.** No document says what took Jacob.
- **The blood is never discussed.** It is cleaned, and it comes back.
- Jacob says almost nothing, and when he does it is short, lower case, and an
  instruction — until Night 6, when he says a great deal and it is worse.
- Tasks stay mundane in their wording even when the night is not.
- **The tune is four notes and it is always the same recording**: the radio on
  Night 1, the kid in the dark and in the car on Night 4, the phone on Night 5,
  the radio on Night 6, the corridor at the end. It is never remarked upon
  until the very last time.
- *Author's standing note: where a scene is thin on dialogue, add some.*

## 9. What was retired, and why it is not coming back

| Was | Now |
|---|---|
| Wren Alcott, sister | **Danny**, and he is the one in the file |
| Danny Alcott, her brother, attendant 1989 | **Jacob**, his friend, and Danny is the player |
| The Count; serving releases arrivals | the disappearances, the salt, and the loop |
| Form 41-C, the pre-typed name | *retired — the desk is disabled* |
| Three 1989 VHS tapes | *retired — the deck is unreachable* |
| 1979 district memo, 1972 tanker clipping | *retired — the cabinet was rebuilt* |
| Three endings decided by a scoring function | **one ending**, the author's |

The station, the job, the six-night structure, the small named cast and the
rule that horror arrives *through the work rather than beside it* were all
kept, because they are load-bearing and none of them contradict this.
